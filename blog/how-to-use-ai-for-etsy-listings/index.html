Etsy & shop tips

How to Use AI for Etsy Listings Without Sounding Generic

By Traci Anderson · November 20, 2026

If you've pasted a product into a chatbot and gotten back "Introducing the perfect addition to your home," you already know the problem. The listing sounds like everyone else's. It invents details you never said. And you still have to rewrite most of it.

I'm a maker, not a tech person. I use an AI helper in my shop so I can get a first draft of a title, tags, and description on the days a blank box just sits there. The helper does not get to publish. I do.

This is the method I actually use, with copy-paste prompts you can try today. It works in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or any similar chatbot. Change the parts in [square brackets] to match what you sell.

Start with a fact sheet, not a vibe

Generic listings happen when we ask a bot to "write an Etsy listing" and give it almost nothing true to work with. It fills the gaps with fluffy words and, sometimes, claims that aren't true.

Before you ask for a title, write down what is actually true: the item, materials, size, what's included, who it's for, how it's used, care, and any shipping or processing notes you already use. If you don't know a fact, leave it blank. Don't let the bot guess.

Paste this and fill in the brackets:

Copy-paste prompt 1
You're helping me draft shop copy. Use only the facts I list. If something is missing, ask me — don't invent it. Don't use hype words like "perfect," "stunning," "must-have," or "luxury" unless I wrote them. Draft only: I will paste, upload, and publish everything myself.

Product fact sheet:
- Item: [what it is]
- Materials: [list only what you know]
- Size / dimensions: [ ]
- Colors / options: [ ]
- What's included: [ ]
- Who it's for / how it's used: [ ]
- Care: [ ]
- Processing or shipping notes I already use: [ ]
- Words I never use: [ ]
- Did AI help make the item itself? [yes / no / partly]. If yes, I will add the disclosure Etsy currently asks for. Do not write a disclosure unless I ask.

Reply with a cleaned-up fact sheet and a short list of anything I still need to fill in.

That cleaned-up sheet becomes the only source for the listing. I keep it in the same chat so later drafts stay honest.

Ask for a listing kit, and tell it the limits

Etsy titles and tags have character limits, and 13 tags is the usual set people work with. A useful draft respects those limits, stays inside your facts, and flags what's missing instead of padding.

Copy-paste prompt 2
Using only the fact sheet above, draft a listing kit:

1) Three title options, each 140 characters or fewer. Put the main item and important facts first. No filler phrases.
2) Exactly 13 tags. Each one 20 characters or fewer, no repeats, no trademarked brand names I didn't list.
3) A short, plain description: what it is, who it's for, what's included, size and materials, care, and how to order options if I listed them. Short paragraphs, no slogans.
4) A "needs from me" list for anything you couldn't write from the fact sheet.

Do not add benefits, materials, or shipping promises I didn't give you.

Pick the title that sounds like you. Read the description out loud. If a sentence could apply to a hundred other shops, cut it or replace it with a fact from your sheet.

Do a second pass just for invented claims

This is the step that stops me from publishing something I can't stand behind. Run it before you copy anything into Etsy.

Copy-paste prompt 3
Review the listing kit against the fact sheet.

List every word or claim in the titles, tags, or description that is not clearly supported by the fact sheet — materials, size, benefits, "handmade" or "custom" language, shipping or processing times, and anything that sounds like a promise.

Then give me a revised kit with those parts removed or turned into questions for me. Keep the same limits: titles under 140 characters, 13 tags of 20 characters or fewer.

If the bot "found" a benefit you like, that's fine — add it to the fact sheet only if it's true, then regenerate. Don't leave a pretty sentence in just because it sounds good.

Draft a pin or caption from the same facts

Once the listing feels solid, I use the same fact sheet for a short pin title and description, or a social caption. Same rule: it drafts, I post.

Copy-paste prompt 4
From the same fact sheet and the title I chose ([paste title]), draft:

- One Pinterest title under 100 characters
- One pin description under 300 characters, written like a person, with a clear next step ("See the listing" is enough)
- Two short social captions under 50 words, no more than 3 hashtags each

Stay inside the facts. Don't ask anyone to "shop the look" or "click the link in bio" unless I use those. Draft only; I will post if I like one.

You don't need a new tool for this. It's one more paste in the same chat.

A warm reply, if a buyer asks a question

I don't let a bot talk to customers. I do let it draft a short reply I can edit, especially when I'm in a rush or the question is one I've answered a hundred times.

Copy-paste prompt 5
Draft a short, warm reply to this buyer message: [paste the message].

Use only details from the fact sheet or this order note: [optional notes]. If I don't have the answer, say so and give me a question to ask them instead of guessing. Under 80 words. No discount or refund language unless I included it. Draft only; I will send it.

If the message is a complaint, a payment issue, or anything about money, I skip the bot and handle it myself.

Safer setup

Nothing should go live, get sent, or get paid without your OK. Don't paste passwords, API keys, or your Etsy login into a chat. If you ever connect a shop tool to a helper, keep approvals on and read every change before it publishes.

If AI helped create the item, check Etsy's current rules about AI-made work and disclosures before you list it. Those rules can change, so I treat them as something to look up, not something a prompt can settle for me.
