Bills & due dates

How to Keep Track of Bills with AI (Without Letting It Pay Anything)

By Traci Anderson · November 20, 2026

Late fees are usually a due date that lived in a portal, an email, or a pile of mail, and never made it onto a list I look at. I don't want a bot that pays my bills. I want one that helps me see what's due, what's repeating, and what I should look at before it renews.

An AI helper is good at turning a messy list into a calendar. It should not get your bank login, and it should not press pay.

These prompts work in ChatGPT or any similar chatbot. This is organizing help, not financial advice. You still pay everything yourself.

Put the safety rules at the top

I paste this first so the chat doesn't offer to pay or cancel for me.

Copy-paste prompt 1
You're my bills-and-due-dates helper. Your job is to list, sort, and remind me. You never pay, schedule a payment, cancel, downgrade, dispute, or contact a company. If I ask you to do one of those things, remind me I do it myself.

Never ask for or store passwords, full account numbers, routing numbers, Social Security numbers, or one-time codes. If I paste a number that looks like an account or card number, tell me to delete it and refer to it only by the last 4 digits.

If a charge looks unfamiliar, flag it for me to check. Don't tell me it's safe or a scam. I'll verify it myself.

If you use a bank or card export, copy only the date, the merchant name, and the amount into the chat. Leave the rest off the page.

Build a tracker from the list you already have

You don't need a perfect spreadsheet to start. A list in your own words is enough: the bill, the usual amount, the due date, and how you pay it.

Copy-paste prompt 2
Here are my recurring bills and subscriptions, in my own words:
[Name — usual amount or a range — due day or "I don't know" — autopay or I pay it — account ending in 1234 if I want a reminder]

Make a bill tracker as a table with these columns: name, usual amount, how often (monthly / quarterly / yearly / not sure), due day if I gave one, how I pay, and a "check this" note if anything is missing.

Then give me a month-at-a-glance for [this month / next month]: each bill under the week it is due, plus a total for the month. If I didn't give a due day, list it at the bottom as "date needed" — don't guess a date.

If you only know some of the dates, that's fine. Look those up on a statement or the company's account page, then tell the bot the real due day.

Use a redacted statement to catch what you forgot

The bills we forget are often the quiet ones: an annual charge, a trial that ended, a subscription under a name you don't recognize. To spot those, paste a few months of redacted transactions — date, merchant, amount only.

Copy-paste prompt 3
I'm going to paste transactions from my bank or card, with account numbers removed. Each line is date, merchant, amount.

Please:
1) List anything that looks like a recurring bill or subscription, with the amount and how often it seems to repeat.
2) Put one-time or unclear charges in a "check with me" list. Don't guess.
3) Flag anything that looks like a trial, a renewal, or a price that changed, and tell me why you flagged it.
4) Do not repeat any full account numbers if I accidentally left one in.

Don't contact anyone. Don't tell me to cancel yet — just show me the list so I can decide.

Confirm every recurring item yourself. Merchant names can be cryptic, and a charge that showed up once might be annual.

Review subscriptions — then you make the call

A good list often shows things you forgot you were still paying for. I use a second prompt to sort them, then I cancel in the company's own account or app.

Copy-paste prompt 4
From the tracker, make a subscription review.

Group them as: keep, look at more closely, or "I may not use this." For the look-at and may-not-use groups, give me one question to answer (Do I still use this? Is there a cheaper plan I already know about?) and the next step I would take myself, like "log in and check the renewal date."

Don't draft cancellation emails unless I ask, and even then, I'll send them. Don't call anything a waste.

If you do decide to cancel, do it through the official channel and mark the date on your tracker. Deleting an app is not the same as canceling.

Turn the list into reminders you control

A tracker only helps if you see it before the due date. I ask the bot for a reminder list I can copy into my phone calendar.

Copy-paste prompt 5
Using the tracker, make a reminder list I can copy into my own calendar.

For each bill I pay myself, remind me [3] days before the due date (or the business day before, if that matters for [rent / a transfer that takes time]). For autopay bills, remind me [3] days before so I can check that the money will be there and the amount looks normal.

Format each line as: date to remind me, bill name, usual amount, how I pay, due date. Then give me a 10-minute monthly checklist I can do on the [1st]: look at the upcoming month, update any amounts that changed, and mark anything unfamiliar.

I will add these reminders myself. Don't create calendar events or send email unless I ask and then approve each one.

Set the reminders on your phone or calendar. If a tool offers to add them, read each one before you say yes.

Safer setup

Never paste full card numbers, bank logins, routing numbers, Social Security numbers, or one-time codes into a chat. Last 4 digits are enough if you need to tell two accounts apart.

The helper lists and reminds. You pay, cancel, dispute, and call the company. If a charge looks wrong, check it on the official statement or with the bank — not with a chatbot's guess.

This is a way to see your due dates more clearly. It isn't financial advice, and it isn't a promise you won't get a late fee.
