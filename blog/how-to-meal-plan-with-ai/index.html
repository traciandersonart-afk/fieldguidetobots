Family & meals

How to Meal Plan with AI When You're Tired of "What's for Dinner?

By Traci Anderson · November 20, 2026

"What's for dinner?" is a small question that somehow takes up a whole corner of my head. By Thursday I have used up my ideas, someone has a thing after school, and I am staring into the fridge hoping a meal appears.

I don't need a fancy meal-planning app. I need five dinners that fit real life, and one grocery list I can take to the store. An AI helper is surprisingly good at that first draft — if you tell it the rules of your house, and you keep the final say.

These prompts work in ChatGPT or any similar chatbot. Fill in the brackets, paste, and edit what you get. The bot does not order the groceries. You do.

Tell it how your house actually eats

A meal plan fails when the bot assumes you cook like a food blog. Give it the boring, useful facts first: how many people, what you will not eat, how much time you have on a weeknight, where you shop, and a budget you can live with.

Copy-paste prompt 1
You're my meal-planning helper. Draft only: I shop, cook, and order everything myself. Never ask for my store login or card number.

Household:
- People eating dinner: [number and ages, if that changes portions]
- Allergies and foods we do not eat: [list]
- Things I will actually cook on a weeknight: [examples: sheet-pan, pasta, tacos]
- Things I will not cook: [examples: recipes with 18 ingredients, seafood, long braises]
- Time on weeknights: [minutes]
- Store: [store name]
- Weekly food budget, dinners only: about $[amount]
- What we already have: [pantry and fridge list, even a messy one]

If I leave a blank, ask me one question. Don't assume an allergy I didn't list, and don't invent a diet plan.

Save this at the top of the chat and reuse it all month. When someone suddenly hates broccoli, just tell the bot and keep going.

Ask for five dinners and one list, not a stack of recipes

I ask for a week I can see on one screen: the meal, a short why-it-fits line, and a single grocery list grouped the way I walk the store. Leftovers count as a win.

Copy-paste prompt 2
Plan 5 dinners for this week using the household notes above.

Rules:
- Max [30] minutes of hands-on time on weeknights. One slightly longer meal is okay on [Saturday / Sunday].
- Stay around $[budget] total for these dinners.
- Reuse ingredients so I'm not buying a jar I'll use once.
- Include one leftovers night or a "build-your-own" night (tacos, baked potatoes, leftovers bowls).
- Give a swap for the pickiest eater if I listed one, without cooking a second meal from scratch.

For each dinner: name, the main ingredients, and the time. Then give me one grocery list grouped by store section, skipping what we already have. Don't add snacks or breakfast unless I ask. Don't place an order.

If the plan looks like a restaurant menu, say so: "Make these more like what we already eat. Fewer new ingredients." The second draft is usually the one I tape to the fridge.

Use what's already in the fridge

The most useful prompt, on a Sunday or a random Wednesday, is the one that starts with leftovers and the slightly sad produce drawer.

Copy-paste prompt 3
Here's what's in the fridge and pantry right now: [list, including leftovers and anything that will go bad soon].

Suggest 3 dinners I can make in under [30] minutes with this, plus the smallest possible add-on list (5 items or fewer). If something I have doesn't fit, it's okay to leave it out. Flag anything that looks like it shouldn't be used if it's old — I'll decide. Don't invent ingredients I didn't list as "already here."

This is also a kind prompt for busy weeks. A "real" meal plan can be soup, toasted sandwiches, and a big salad. It still counts.

Plan the dinners around the actual week

A beautiful meal plan dies when Tuesday is a late practice and you were going to start a roast. Paste the calendar, even roughly.

Copy-paste prompt 4
Here's this week's schedule, in plain words: [Mon: late practice 6:15. Tue: home by 5. Wed: school project due, I need a 20-minute meal. Thu: leftovers okay. Fri: pizza is fine.]

Revise the 5-dinner plan and the grocery list to fit this week. Put the easiest meals on the hardest nights. If a meal no longer fits, replace it and tell me what to drop from the list and what to add. Keep the same budget and allergies.

I also use this when the week changes midstream. "We didn't make the chicken. Rebuild Thursday and Friday with what's left."

Safer setup

The helper drafts a plan and a list. It should not order groceries, charge a card, or message anyone unless you clearly ask and then approve it.

Put allergies and "we do not eat this" in your own words, and check the plan yourself — especially for anyone with an allergy. This is meal planning help, not nutrition advice or a medical diet. If someone in the house is on a plan from a doctor or dietitian, follow that, not a chatbot.

Don't paste store passwords, membership numbers, or your shopping-app login into the chat.
