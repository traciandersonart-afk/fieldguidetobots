Etsy messages

How to Reply to Etsy Customer Messages with AI (and Still Sound Like You)

By Traci Anderson · November 24, 2026

I love getting Etsy messages. I also leave them sitting for two days because my ADHD brain decides the reply needs to be perfect, and then I forget, and then I feel weird about it. Meanwhile the buyer just wanted to know if the print comes in 8x10.

An AI helper fixes the part where I stare at a blank box. It can turn my facts into a friendly first draft in a few seconds. It doesn't know my shop, my shipping, or whether I'm willing to make that custom pet portrait with a tiny crown. I do. So the bot drafts, and I read, fix, and hit send myself.

These prompts work in ChatGPT or any similar chatbot. Fill in the brackets, paste, and edit what you get.

Give it your shop facts first

Most robot-sounding replies happen because the bot had nothing true to work with, so it made up something polite and vague. Paste this once at the top of a chat (or save it in a note on your phone).

Copy-paste prompt 1
You're my Etsy message helper. Draft only. I read, edit, and send every message myself.

My shop: [shop name], I sell [what you make].
Processing time: [___ business days]. Shipping: [how and about how long].
Returns and exchanges: [your policy, in your words].
Custom orders: [yes / no / only these kinds].

Use only these facts. If a reply needs something that isn't here, leave a [BRACKET] for me to fill in instead of guessing. Never promise refunds, discounts, rush shipping, or dates I didn't give you.

That bracket line is the whole trick. A blank bracket is annoying for two seconds. A made-up shipping date is annoying for two weeks.

Teach it how you actually talk

My messages sound like me: short, a little goofy, maybe one exclamation point if I'm feeling wild. Generic AI replies sound like a hotel front desk. Fix that with a few of your own old messages.

Copy-paste prompt 2
Here are 3 messages I've sent buyers that sound like me:
[paste 3 replies, with buyer names removed]

Describe how I write in 4 short bullet points: sentence length, how I start and end, words I use, and words I never use. Use those rules for every reply in this chat. No "I hope this message finds you well," no corporate words, and no more than one exclamation point.

If the bullets don't sound like you, tell it. "I would never say 'delighted'" is a perfectly good correction.

Draft a reply to a real message

Now paste the actual message. Leave out the buyer's full name, address, and order number; the bot doesn't need them to write a nice reply.

Copy-paste prompt 3
A buyer sent this: "[paste the message, without their full name or address]"

What I want to say: [your answer in a few words, like "yes, 8x10 is available, ships in 3 days"].

Draft a short, warm reply (under 80 words) in my voice. Answer the question in the first sentence. Leave a [BRACKET] for anything you're not sure about.

"What I want to say" keeps you in charge. The bot is only dressing up your answer, not deciding it.

Say yes or no to a custom request

Custom requests are where I overthink the most. Is "no" rude? Is "yes" a trap? Decide first, then let the bot word it.

Copy-paste prompt 4
A buyer asked for this custom request: "[paste it]"

My decision: [yes / no / yes with changes].
If yes: price [$___ or "I'll quote it"], timeline [___], and what I need from them [photos, names, colors].
If no: one kind sentence on why, and [a similar item I already sell, if any].

Draft a clear, friendly reply in my voice. Don't apologize more than once.

A clear no is kinder than a mushy maybe. And a yes with the price and timeline right there saves three more rounds of messages.

Build a little set of quick replies

Etsy lets you save pre-written answers as quick replies (they used to be called saved replies), right in Messages. This is where AI saves real time, because you only write each one once.

Copy-paste prompt 5
Using my shop facts and my voice rules, draft short quick-reply templates for the messages I get most:
1. Where's my order? (with a spot for tracking)
2. Can this be customized?
3. What size is it / will it fit my frame?
4. Thanks for your order (no review begging)
5. A polite no for something I don't make

Keep each under 60 words. Put a [BRACKET] anywhere I need to add a detail. Label each one.

Read each one, fix anything that isn't true for your shop, and paste the keepers into Etsy yourself. Then you personalize the bracket parts before sending, every time.

Do a calm check on a cranky message

Some messages sting. I don't let the bot run those. If it's about a refund, a payment, a damaged order, or an Etsy case, I decide what happens and I write the reply myself. Where the bot helps is checking my draft before I send it, because a tired, grumpy maker writes a different email than a rested one.

Copy-paste prompt 6
Here's a reply I wrote to an upset buyer: "[paste your draft]"

Don't change what I'm offering or add anything new. Just tell me if any sentence sounds defensive, snippy, or confusing, and suggest a calmer version of only those sentences. Keep it in my voice.

"Don't change what I'm offering" matters. You want a tone check, not a bot quietly adding a free replacement you never agreed to.

Safer setup

The helper drafts. You send. That goes for every reply, every quick reply, and every custom quote.

Don't paste buyers' full names, addresses, emails, order numbers, or payment details into a chat. First names or "the buyer" are plenty. Don't give a chatbot your Etsy password or let any tool log in and answer messages for you.

Read every draft before it goes out. Check the facts against your actual listing and policies, because a bot will happily promise a size you don't sell. Anything about money, refunds, or a case, you decide and you write. Nothing gets sent, refunded, or changed in your shop without your OK.
