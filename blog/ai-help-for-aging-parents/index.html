Family caregivers

How to Use AI When You're Helping Aging Parents

By Traci Anderson · November 20, 2026

Helping a parent can turn into a second job you never applied for: appointments, forms, a family group text, and a running list in your head of things you don't want to forget. I looked at AI for this because I needed a way to sort the work, not because I wanted a bot to make the big decisions.

It can be a good organizing assistant. It is a poor doctor, lawyer, or financial advisor. The difference matters.

These prompts are for the paperwork-and-planning part of helping — the lists, the "what should I ask on Thursday," the draft of a careful conversation. They are not medical, legal, or financial advice. They work in ChatGPT or any similar chatbot. Involve your parent whenever you can. It's their life.

Set the rules, and take the identifiers out

Before you paste anything, remove details that would identify your parent to a stranger: full legal names, date of birth, address, Social Security or Medicare numbers, insurance IDs, and medical record numbers. "Mom," "the clinic," and "Thursday at 2" are enough for a list.

I also paste a short rule so the bot stays in its lane.

Copy-paste prompt 1
You're an organization helper for a family caregiver. Draft only: I will call, send, book, and decide everything myself.

Do not diagnose, suggest medications or doses, interpret test results, or tell me what a doctor "would" do. Do not give legal or financial advice. If I ask something that's really a medical, legal, or benefits question, help me write the question for the right professional instead of answering it as fact.

I've removed last names, ID numbers, and addresses. If I slip and paste one, remind me to delete it and don't repeat it back in full.

Keep answers short. Flag anything you assumed.

If your parent is okay with the idea, tell them what you're using the helper for. Some people want to be in the chat.

Turn a messy pile of notes into a task list

The first useful job is getting the open loops out of your head and onto a list with an owner. You decide if the owners are right.

Copy-paste prompt 2
Turn these de-identified notes into a task list. Don't add medical advice or invent facts I didn't write.

Notes: [paste your notes]

Make a table with four columns: task, suggested owner (me / sibling / parent / clinic — only if the notes support it), a possible timing, and the very next action. Group anything urgent at the top. If you're not sure, put it under "Check with me" instead of guessing.

This is also handy after a long phone call, when you have half a page of scribbles and no idea what is actually first.

Walk into an appointment with better questions

I don't ask a chatbot what's wrong. I do ask it to help me turn a doctor's written instructions, or the things we want to remember to ask, into plain language and a short question list. Then I check that list against the paper I already have, and I ask the clinician — not the bot.

Copy-paste prompt 3
Here are the written instructions or notes from a recent visit (I've removed names and ID numbers): [paste].

1) Restate them in plain language a tired person can follow, without adding new medical advice.
2) List anything that is unclear or missing, as questions I can ask the clinic.
3) Give me 5 short questions for the next appointment, based only on these notes and this concern: [the thing you want to bring up].

If you aren't sure, say so. I will verify everything with the doctor or pharmacist before we change any routine.

Bring the output as a draft. "The chatbot said" is not a reason to change a medication, skip a visit, or start a new treatment.

Draft a hard conversation, then say it in your own voice

Talking about driving, a new helper in the house, or moving appointments around can go sideways fast. A bot can help you practice a kind opening. It cannot read the room for you.

Copy-paste prompt 4
Help me draft a conversation with my parent about [topic: for example, nighttime driving / having a helper on Tuesdays / bringing a sibling into the loop].

What I know about how they like to talk: [values, what usually helps, what shuts the door].
The outcome I want: [a small, specific next step — not "they agree with me"].
I will edit this and say it myself.

Give me: one opening I could actually say, a response if they get defensive, a response if they go quiet, and a way to pause the talk without ending it forever. No scare tactics. No talking about them as if they aren't in the room.

Read it out loud. If it doesn't sound like you, rewrite it until it does. The point is a first draft, not a script you have to follow.

Make a one-page week, and a short handoff

When more than one person is helping, things get dropped in the cracks. A simple week view — and a short handoff after a visit — is often more useful than a long care "plan" a bot invented.

Copy-paste prompt 5
Make a one-page weekday overview from these facts only: [wake time, meals, standing appointments, who is stopping by, rides needed, and any tasks a person has to do]. Leave open time. Mark anything that needs a helper in the house.

Then write a 10-line handoff I could text to the next family member: what was done, what changed, and what's next. No medical advice. If a fact is missing, ask me; don't fill it in.

You stay the editor. Check times, rides, and anything about medication against the labels and the care team, not against the chatbot.

Safer setup

A consumer chatbot is not a doctor, not an emergency service, and not covered like a clinic is. Don't paste Social Security numbers, Medicare or insurance IDs, bank logins, or full medical records into a chat.

Nothing should be sent, posted, booked, or paid without your OK. Don't let a helper email siblings, call a clinic, or fill a form for you unless you asked, reviewed it, and approved it.

For anything you'll act on — symptoms, medications, benefits, legal papers — verify with a person whose job that is: a clinician, a pharmacist, an elder-law attorney, or your local Area Agency on Aging. If it's an emergency, call local emergency services, not a chatbot.
